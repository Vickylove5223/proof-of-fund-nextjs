The key requirements for Nigerians traveling abroad for work include a valid international passport, a work visa or permit, an official employment contract, verifiable Proof of Funds (POF), a police clearance certificate, a medical examination report, comprehensive travel insurance, pre-departure orientation, and a clear understanding of your tax obligations.

## The Essential Checklist: 10 Requirements for Working Abroad

Think of this as your master list. Ticking off each item brings you one step closer to your international career goal.

### 1 A Valid Nigerian International Passport

This is your primary identity document and is non-negotiable. Most countries require your passport to be valid for at least six months beyond your intended stay.

Ensure your passport has enough blank pages for visas and entry/exit stamps.

![Work Visa](/images/finance-2.png)

### 2 The Correct Work Visa or Permit

A tourist visa won’t cut it. You need a specific work visa or permit issued by your destination country.

The application process, timeline, and required documents vary significantly from one country to another. Always check the official embassy or consulate website for the most accurate information.

### 3 Confirmed Employment Contract or Offer Letter

This document is proof of your job offer. It must be on the company’s official letterhead and should clearly state your role, responsibilities, salary, duration of employment, and other terms and conditions.

This letter is a cornerstone of your visa application.

![Proof of Funds Document](/images/finance-3.png)

### 5 Police Clearance Certificate (PCC)

Most countries require a PCC to verify that you do not have a criminal record. In Nigeria, you can obtain this certificate from the Nigeria Police Force (NPF).

The process usually involves submitting an application and getting your fingerprints captured.

![Medical Examination](/images/finance-1.png)

### 6 Medical Examination and Vaccination Records

To protect public health, many countries require a comprehensive medical examination from an approved panel physician. This checks for communicable diseases and ensures you are medically fit to work.

You’ll also need proof of required vaccinations, often documented in a ‘Yellow Card’. is an excellent resource.

### 7 Comprehensive Travel and Health Insurance

While your employer might provide health insurance once you start working, many countries require you to have your own travel and health insurance to cover the initial period. This protects you from unforeseen medical emergencies and travel-related issues like lost luggage or flight cancellations.

This is especially important for popular destinations like the [UK and other top migration routes](/guides/how-to-japa-from-nigeria-to-uk-5-key-migration-routes).

![Pre-Departure Orientation](/images/finance-2.png)

### 8 Pre-Departure Orientation or Training

Some visa categories or employers may require you to attend a pre-departure orientation seminar. provide valuable information about your destination country’s culture, laws, and workplace ethics.

### 9 Tax Clearance and Financial Obligations

It’s wise to ensure your financial affairs are in order before you leave. to show that you have fulfilled your tax obligations in Nigeria.

Proper [narration for your financial documentation](/guides/12-best-narration-for-proof-of-funds-in-nigeria) can be crucial.

![Flight Itinerary](/images/finance-3.png)

### 10 Accommodation and Flight Itinerary

You don’t always need to buy a flight ticket before your visa is approved, but you will need a flight reservation or itinerary. Similarly, you should have proof of your initial accommodation, whether it’s a temporary hotel booking or a letter from your employer confirming arrangements.
