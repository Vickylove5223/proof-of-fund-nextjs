Understanding Austria’s Proof of Funds Requirement
--------------------------------------------------

The Austrian government wants to ensure that international students can support themselves financially throughout their studies without needing to work excessively or rely on state support. Therefore, you must prove you have a certain amount of money accessible to you for your first year.

According to the official Austrian Agency for Education and Internationalisation (OeAD), the required amounts for 2024 are:

*   **Students up to 24 years old:** €672.64 per month (€8,071.68 for a full year).
*   **Students over 24 years old:** €1,217.96 per month (€14,615.52 for a full year).

Acceptable Types of Proof of Funds
----------------------------------

Austria accepts several forms of financial proof. You can choose the one that best suits your situation.

### Personal Bank Account

This is the most common method. You’ll need to provide a bank statement from a savings or deposit account in your name.

The embassy will want to see that the funds have been in the account for a reasonable period (typically 3-6 months) to show they are genuinely yours and not just deposited for the visa application.

### Sponsor’s Account

If a sponsor (like a parent or guardian) is funding your education, you can use their financial documents. You will need to provide:

*   Their official bank statements.
*   A formal letter of sponsorship or declaration of commitment, stating they will cover your expenses.
*   Proof of your relationship with the sponsor (e.g., a birth certificate).

### Blocked Account (Sperrkonto)

A blocked account is a special type of bank account where the total amount for the year is deposited at once. A fixed monthly amount is then released to you after you arrive in Austria.

This is considered a very strong and reliable form of POF by the embassy because it guarantees your financial stability.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
------------------------

A visa rejection can be heartbreaking, and POF issues are a common cause. Here are some mistakes to avoid:

*   **Sudden Large Deposits:** A large, unexplained deposit made just before applying can raise red flags. The embassy needs to see a consistent financial history.
*   **Insufficient Funds:** Make sure you have the exact required amount, plus a small buffer for currency fluctuations.
*   **Inauthentic Documents:** Never use fake or altered bank statements. Embassies have strict verification processes.
*   **Unclear Source of Funds:** Be prepared to explain where the money came from (e.g., salary, sale of property, educational savings).
