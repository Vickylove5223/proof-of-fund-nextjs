### 1. Canada (IRCC) Bank Statement Rules

The Canadian government is heavily focused on transaction history and liquidity.
*   Duration Required: You must provide statements covering the past 4 to 6 months.
*   The Big Rule: They want to see a natural progression of funds. Sudden, unexplained large deposits (lump sums) will likely lead to refusal unless accompanied by a Gift Deed or a logical Letter of Explanation.
*   Format: Standard bank statements printed on official letterheads or digitally stamped PDFs.

### 2. United Kingdom (UKVI) Bank Statement Rules

The UK takes a much more rigid, mathematical approach, especially for students and workers.
*   Duration Required: For Students and Skilled Workers, the required funds must have been held for a minimum of 28 consecutive days. For Visitors, 3 to 6 months of history is preferred.
*   The Big Rule: During the 28-day period, the balance must never drop below the required amount. Not even for a second. Furthermore, the statement must be printed no more than 31 days before the date you submit the application.
*   Format: Highly strict. Must be on official letterhead, show your exact name, account number, and preferably be stamped by the bank if not accompanied by an official verification letter.

### 3. Schengen Visa Bank Statement Rules

Schengen embassies (France, Germany, Italy, etc.) are deeply concerned with daily subsistence and regular income.
*   Duration Required: You must provide exactly 3 to 6 months of statements. 
*   The Big Rule: You must prove you have a specific minimum amount of euros per day for your trip (e.g., €120/day for France). However, they also scrutinize the account for regular salary deposits. If your account shows no regular income but suddenly has ₦10 Million for a trip, they will refuse the visa under "lack of economic ties to home country."
*   Format: Must usually be stamped on every single page by the bank manager.
