### Does the Embassy Accept an OPay Bank Statement?

The short and blunt answer is NO. You should never use an OPay bank statement as your primary Proof of Funds for major international visas (like the UKVI or IRCC). 

While OPay is fully licensed by the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) as a Mobile Money Operator, international embassies have entirely different requirements when assessing financial credibility. 

Here is why submitting an OPay statement will likely result in a visa refusal:

#### 1. The "Mobile Money" Classification
Embassies require funds to be held in traditional, Tier-1 Commercial Banks. Digital wallets and fintech apps (like OPay, PalmPay, or Moniepoint) are often flagged by visa officers because they are designed for rapid, informal transactions rather than long-term asset holding. 

#### 2. International Verification Issues
When a visa officer in London or Ottawa reviews your application, they often need to verify the bank statement directly with the issuing institution. Traditional commercial banks have dedicated corporate desks for international verification. Digital wallets often lack the infrastructure to respond to embassy verification emails promptly, leading to an automatic refusal.

#### 3. Format and Presentation
Embassies require bank statements to be printed on official letterheads and often physically stamped by a branch manager. Because OPay is a digital-first platform without traditional banking halls, obtaining a physically stamped, embassy-compliant document is extremely difficult.
