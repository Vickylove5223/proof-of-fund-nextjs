### **Liquid Funds vs. Fixed Assets**

It is important to understand the difference. For a Canadian PR, Study Permit, or Italian Visa, the embassy requires "Settlement Funds"—money that is readily available to spend on rent, food, and tuition.

*   **Fixed Assets (Land/Houses):** These are not liquid. You cannot buy a food with it in Toronto by showing a C of O for a plot of land in Lekki.
    
*   **Liquid Funds:** Cash in a savings or checking account that you can withdraw at any moment.
    

### **How to Effectively Use Property in Your Application**

Even though you can't use a house as your primary POF, you should still include your property documents for these two powerful reasons:

#### **1\. Proving Source of Wealth**

If a large sum of money suddenly appears in your account, the best way to explain it is through a **Property Sale**.

*   **Required Documents:** Include the Deed of Sale, receipts of payment, and the bank credit notification showing the inflow from the buyer. This turns a "suspicious lump sum" into a legitimate financial transaction.
    

#### **2\. Establishing Strong Home Ties**

One of the most common reasons for visa refusal is "I am not satisfied that you will return to your home country." Owning a multi-million Naira property in Nigeria is one of the strongest proofs that you have a reason to come back.

*   **Required Documents:** Certificate of Occupancy (C of O), Governor’s Consent, or a Registered Deed of Assignment. Including these shows you are well-established and unlikely to abscond.
    

### **The "Gift Deed" Property Strategy**

If a parent is gifting you the proceeds from a land sale to fund your Japa journey, you must document it correctly.

*   The parent should provide the land documents to show where the money came from.
    
*   They must then provide a **Deed of Gift** stating the money is for your relocation and is non-repayable.
