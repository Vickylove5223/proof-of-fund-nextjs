Thousands of Nigerian students build their study-abroad plans on one assumption: *"I'll fund it with an international student loan."* For 2026, that assumption needs urgent updating — **Prodigy Finance has paused support for African applicants for the Fall 2026 intake**, removing one of the two big no-collateral lenders from the table.

The good news: loans remain a legitimate, embassy-accepted way to satisfy proof of funds — if you use the right lender and present the right paperwork. This guide covers who still lends to Nigerians, how a loan letter works as POF for each destination, and the timing traps.

## Can a Loan Really Serve as Proof of Funds?

Yes. Immigration authorities in the US, UK, and Canada all accept **education loans from regulated lenders** as financial evidence:

*   **USA (F1):** a loan approval/sanction letter counts toward the I-20 amount and is standard at interview — pair it with the rest of your [F1 financial evidence](/usa-f1-visa-proof-of-funds-nigeria-sponsor-letter-complete-guide)
*   **UK (Student visa):** a loan letter from a regulated lender in your name satisfies maintenance — one of the few alternatives to the [28-day cash rule](/common-mistakes-in-28-day-proof-of-funds-for-uk-visas-and-how-to-avoid-them)
*   **Canada:** education loans are listed by IRCC as acceptable proof — they combine with the [other elements of a 2026 Canada file](/canada-proof-of-funds-2026-new-amounts-in-naira-study-permit-express-entry)

The loan must be **approved and documented** — a pending application proves nothing.

## Who Still Lends to Nigerian Students in 2026

### MPower Financing — the main no-collateral option

*   **$2,001 – $100,000**, no collateral, no co-signer, 10-year terms
*   For students within two years of graduating at ~400 approved schools in the **US and Canada**
*   Issues the support letters and disbursement confirmations universities and embassies expect
*   Details: [MPower's Nigeria page](https://www.mpowerfinancing.com/financial-empowerment/student-loans-nigeria)

### GlobCred

*   Loan structures **explicitly recognised by UKVI, IRCC, and US embassies** for proof of funds
*   Covers tuition and living costs; works with UK destinations MPower doesn't
*   Details: [GlobCred Nigeria](https://globcred.money/nigeria-student-loan)

### Prodigy Finance — paused for Africa, Fall 2026

Prodigy's pause covers African applicants for the Fall 2026 intake ([their Africa page](https://prodigyfinance.com/home-country/africa/)). If Prodigy was your plan, you need a replacement now — pauses like this historically outlast one intake. MPower (US/Canada masters programmes) is the closest substitute; GlobCred covers UK routes.

### Local options

Nigerian bank education loans and the government student-loan scheme exist, but for *international* POF purposes their letters carry less weight and foreign-currency disbursement is harder to evidence. Treat them as supplements, not the spine of your file.

## The Three Timing Traps

1. **Loan approval comes after admission.** You can't show a loan letter at application time for most lenders — sequence your timeline: admission → loan approval → visa file.
2. **Partial coverage.** Loans often cover tuition but not the full living-cost requirement — the balance still needs [cash evidence with proper history](/proof-of-funds-vs-bank-statement-vs-bank-reference-letter). A Canada file needs the **CAD $22,895** living component covered one way or another.
3. **Conditional approvals.** "Approved subject to visa" letters create a chicken-and-egg problem some embassies probe. Get the letter's conditions in writing and pair it with stronger cash evidence.
