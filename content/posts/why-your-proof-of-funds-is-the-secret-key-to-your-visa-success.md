The Two Biggest Reasons for Visa Denials
----------------------------------------

Statistically, the two most common reasons for visa denials globally are a **lack of strong ties to your home country** and **insufficient funds**. Many applicants believe that simply having a travel agent handle the paperwork is enough. However, travel agents are humans—they make mistakes, and they get paid regardless of your result. The consequence of a "Refusal" stays on your record, not theirs. Consular officers are trained to look for legitimacy. They don't just look at your closing balance; they look at the history of how that money got there. If you cannot explain your source of income, you are likely to be denied under [Section 214(b) of the US Immigration and Nationality Act](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/visa-denials.html).

Building Your POF: Business Owners vs. Salaried Workers
-------------------------------------------------------

### For Business Owners

If you run a business, no matter how small, you must have a registered business account. Mixing personal and business funds is a major red flag. Embassies want to see "Inflow and Outflow." A stagnant account with a large sum of money is suspicious. They want to see that money moves—proving that your business is active and generates consistent revenue.

### For Salaried Employees

The "believability" of your account is key. If you earn N110,000 monthly but your flight ticket costs N1.7 million, a consular officer will assume you are going abroad to "run away" and look for a better life. Your income must logically support the cost of your trip. If your salary can't cover the flight ticket, you need a solid explanation or a specialized financial structure. _Related: [Learn more about Proof of Funds for Study Visas](http://afrikamombraids.local/proof-of-funds-for-study-visa/)_

The Danger of the Exchange Rate Fluctuations
--------------------------------------------

One area where many Nigerians fail is the "Exchange Rate Trap." If you started building your POF six months ago when the dollar was N900, and you saved N10 million, that money might have been enough then. However, if the rate is now N1,500 per dollar at the time of your interview, your N10 million is no longer sufficient to cover your [UK Student Visa maintenance requirements](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/money). You must build or secure your funds based on the **current** exchange rate at the time of your application. Failing to account for Naira devaluation is a leading cause of "Insufficient Funds" refusals.

Don't Risk Your Visa with "Lump Sum" Deposits
---------------------------------------------

Sudden, large deposits (known as "Skaya") into a dormant account a week before your visa interview will almost always lead to a rejection. Embassies know this trick. Whether you are using your money or our service, the transition must be handled professionally.
