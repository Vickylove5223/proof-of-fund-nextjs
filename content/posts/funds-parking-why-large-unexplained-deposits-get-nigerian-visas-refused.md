You finally gathered the money for your visa application. A relative transferred ₦3 million, you moved ₦2 million from your business account, and your statement now shows a healthy balance. Two months later: **refused — "I am not satisfied the funds are genuinely available to you."**

Welcome to the most common financial refusal in Nigerian visa applications: **funds parking**. This guide explains what it is, how visa officers detect it in seconds, and the right way to handle a genuinely large deposit.

## What Is Funds Parking?

Funds parking is when money is moved into an account shortly before a visa application purely to inflate the balance — with the officer suspecting it will be moved right back out after the statement is printed. Visa officers in Lagos and Abuja review hundreds of Nigerian statements weekly; the pattern of a dormant or low-activity account suddenly receiving a large credit is instantly recognisable.

It doesn't matter that the money is "real." What matters is whether it is **genuinely yours and genuinely available** for your trip or studies.

## How Officers Detect It

*   **Deposit timing** — large credits within days or weeks of the application date
*   **Proportion** — a deposit that dwarfs every other transaction in the 6-month history
*   **No economic story** — the account shows ₦180,000/month salary, then a single ₦8m credit with narration like "transfer"
*   **Round figures** — ₦5,000,000.00 exactly, with no kobo, from a personal account
*   **Post-statement checks** — some embassies (especially the UK and Canada) re-verify balances with banks weeks after submission; if the money vanished, so has your credibility

## The Right Way to Handle a Genuine Large Deposit

Large inflows are not automatically fatal — **undocumented** large inflows are. If you sold land, received a contract payment, got a gift from a parent, or liquidated an investment:

1. **Keep the paper trail**: deed of sale, contract invoice, investment redemption letter, or a signed gift deed.
2. **Write a source of funds letter** explaining each major credit. We have [templates and samples for source of funds explanations here](/source-of-funds-explanation-for-pof-loan-nigeria-samples-templates-expert-tips-2026).
3. **Match the narration**: ask the sender to use a clear transfer narration — our guide on the [12 best narrations for proof of funds](/12-best-narration-for-proof-of-funds-in-nigeria) shows what works.
4. **Let it season**: where possible, complete large transfers 3–6 months before applying.
5. **Never backdate anything.** The [consequences of fake or backdated statements](/consequences-of-fake-proof-of-funds-or-backdated-bank-statements-for-visa-applications-from-nigeria-2026) include 10-year bans.
