Understanding Canada Work Permits and POF Requirements
------------------------------------------------------

First, understand the different work permit types; POF requirements depend on the specific permit. The main categories are employer-specific and open work permits.

### Employer-Specific vs. Open Work Permits

An **Employer-Specific Work Permit** allows you to work in Canada under the conditions specified on your permit. These include the employer's name, work duration, and location.

This permit typically requires a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) and a job offer.

An **Open Work Permit** offers greater flexibility, allowing you to work for almost any employer in Canada. Spouses or common-law partners of certain temporary foreign workers or international students are often eligible.



### When is Proof of Funds (POF) Actually Required?

According to IRCC requirements, you must prove you can financially support yourself and your family members upon arrival in Canada.

Proof of Funds is almost always required for **open work permit** applicants. Without a guaranteed job, Canadian officials need assurance you can support yourself while job hunting.

For **employer-specific work permits**, while a job offer indicates income, officers may still request POF to cover initial expenses before your first paycheck.

What is Acceptable as Proof of Funds? -------------------------------------

Canadian immigration authorities are specific about acceptable POF. You must prove the money is yours and readily available.

Acceptable documents include:

*   Official bank statements from your personal accounts covering the past four to six months.
*   A letter from a financial institution on official letterhead, detailing your account balances.
*   Proof of investment portfolios (stocks, bonds).
*   A bank draft or cheque in convertible currency.

Funds must be in your name or your spouse/partner's name. Funds from other family members are only acceptable with a formal letter of sponsorship.
