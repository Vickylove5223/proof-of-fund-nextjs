You got the Certificate of Sponsorship. The job is real, the salary meets the threshold — and then your application stalls on a small financial detail nobody warned you about: **maintenance funds**.

Here is the rule in one line: unless your employer certifies your maintenance, you must show **at least £1,270 (roughly ₦2.5m) held in your personal account for 28 consecutive days**, ending no more than 31 days before your application date.

It is a small amount compared to student visa requirements — and yet it refuses real applications every month, usually on technicalities. This guide covers exactly how to satisfy it from Nigeria.

## The Two Ways to Meet the Requirement

### Option 1: Self-maintenance (your own £1,270)

*   Minimum **£1,270** in your account — savings, not overdraft
*   Held for **28 consecutive days** with the balance never dipping below £1,270, even for one day
*   Evidence dated within **31 days** of your application
*   Acceptable evidence: bank statements or an official bank letter covering the full 28-day window

### Option 2: Sponsor certification

Your UK employer ticks the box on your Certificate of Sponsorship confirming they will "certify maintenance" — guaranteeing your support up to £1,270 for your first month. **If your sponsor certifies, you show nothing.** Ask your employer before you spend weeks seasoning funds; large licensed sponsors (including NHS trusts) certify routinely.

## Care Workers: Read This Section Twice

The Health and Care Worker route uses the same £1,270 rule — but with two realities Nigerians must plan around:

1. **Dependants are restricted.** Since the 2024–2026 rule changes, most new care workers cannot bring partners or children on dependant visas. Budget and plan on this basis.
2. **Where dependants are still allowed** (other Skilled Worker occupations), each adds to the requirement: **£285 for a partner, £315 for the first child, £200 per additional child** — all subject to the same 28-day rule.

| Who | Additional funds |
| --- | --- |
| Main applicant | £1,270 |
| Partner | +£285 |
| First child | +£315 |
| Each further child | +£200 |

A family of four self-maintaining therefore shows **£2,070** held for 28 days.

## The Technicalities That Refuse Applications

*   **The one-day dip.** Balance touched £1,265 on day 19? The 28-day clock restarts. This mirrors the [28-day rule mistakes we see on student visas](/common-mistakes-in-28-day-proof-of-funds-for-uk-visas-and-how-to-avoid-them).
*   **Stale evidence.** Statement ends 35 days before your application date — refused.
*   **Naira accounts without conversion clarity.** Naira balances are acceptable, but the converted value must stay above £1,270 across all 28 days. Exchange-rate swings have refused borderline files — keep a healthy buffer (we suggest maintaining ₦3m+).
*   **Unrecognised institutions.** Use a major commercial bank — see [which Nigerian banks visa officers trust](/top-3-banks-for-visa-proof-of-funds-in-nigeria-and-how-to-get-pof-without-personal-cash).
*   **Funds that appear suddenly.** Even at this small scale, [funds parking](/funds-parking-why-large-unexplained-deposits-get-nigerian-visas-refused) logic applies.

## Timeline for a Clean Application

1. **Day 0:** Confirm whether your sponsor will certify maintenance. If yes — done.
2. **Day 0–5:** If not, fund your account to ₦3m+ and stop touching it.
3. **Day 28+:** Request stamped statements plus a bank letter covering the full window.
4. **Within 31 days:** Submit your application with the evidence fresh.

Official reference: the [GOV.UK Skilled Worker visa guidance](https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/how-much-it-costs) covers the current figures and rules.
