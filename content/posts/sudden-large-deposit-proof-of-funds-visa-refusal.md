### Why Embassies Hate Sudden Large Deposits

To an immigration officer, your bank statement tells a story about your financial lifestyle. If your historical account balance is usually around ₦150,000, and then a mysterious ₦35 Million appears without explanation, the story breaks. 

The embassy's primary concern is that you cannot actually afford to live and study in their country, which increases the risk of you working illegally or relying on public funds.

### How to Avoid a Lump Sum Refusal

If you are using a third-party service like Proof of Fund NG to secure your financial requirements, it is completely legal, but you must present the funds correctly. Here is how to avoid a sudden large deposit refusal:

#### 1. Provide a Source of Funds Explanation
Never let a large sum sit in your account unexplained. You must provide a Letter of Explanation (LOE). This letter should clearly state where the money came from. Common legitimate explanations include:
*   The sale of property or land.
*   Liquidation of shares or investments.
*   A legal Gift Deed from a sponsor (such as a parent or wealthy relative).

#### 2. Use a Gift Deed (Affidavit of Support)
If the money is being provided by a sponsor, the sudden deposit can be justified with a Gift Deed. A Gift Deed is a legally binding document sworn in a court of law stating that the money is a non-repayable gift meant entirely for your travel and study expenses. (Note: You will need a lawyer to draft and swear this affidavit in court; it is then attached to your bank statement).

### How much money is considered suspicious activity?

For visa applications, any sudden deposit that is wildly disproportionate to your normal historical account balance is considered suspicious activity. For example, if your account normally holds ₦50,000 and suddenly receives a ₦20 Million lump sum without a Gift Deed or Letter of Explanation, the visa officer will immediately flag it as borrowed funds.
