### The 4-to-6 Months History Rule

The IRCC does not have a strict minimum number of days the funds must sit untouched (like the UK's 28 days). Instead, the IRCC explicitly asks for bank statements covering the past 4 to 6 months.

Why? Because the visa officer wants to see your *average balance* and your *transaction history*. They want to understand your financial behavior. 

If your account has maintained a healthy, steady balance over 4 months, the officer can easily conclude that the money genuinely belongs to you. 

### The Danger of Sudden Deposits (Lump Sums)

Because the IRCC looks at a 4-to-6 month history window, simply dumping ₦30 Million into an empty account and printing a statement the next day is incredibly dangerous. 

When a visa officer looks at the historical data and sees that the account was empty for 3 months, only to receive a massive lump sum deposit 48 hours before the visa application, they will assume the money was borrowed. This will lead to a refusal.

### How to Satisfy the IRCC Without Tying Down Your Capital

If you don't have millions of Naira sitting idly in your account for 6 months, how do you legally pass the IRCC financial check? 

1. Provide a Source of Funds Explanation:
If you receive a sudden deposit to cover your POF, you must attach a Letter of Explanation. The most common and legal way to justify a sudden injection of funds is through a Gift Deed (an affidavit sworn in court proving a sponsor gave you the money permanently) or by providing proof of a recent property or land sale.
