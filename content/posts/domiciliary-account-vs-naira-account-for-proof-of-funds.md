It is one of the most-asked questions in every visa WhatsApp group: **"Should I show my money in dollars or naira?"** — usually followed by someone insisting embassies "respect dom accounts more."

Here is the accurate answer: **embassies accept both, and neither earns you extra credibility by itself.** What actually matters is the converted value, the stability of that value through your processing window, and the same verification standards that apply to any account. But there are real strategic differences — and one exchange-rate trap that refuses borderline applications every month.

## How Embassies Treat Naira Balances

Every embassy converts your balance to its own currency at the assessment date — UKVI uses published OANDA rates, IRCC uses standard conversion, and Schengen posts convert to euros. A naira statement is not a weakness; officers in Lagos and Abuja read naira statements all day.

The **trap** is volatility: if you hold exactly the required amount in naira and the naira slides 8% between application and assessment, your file now shows *less than the requirement* — and the refusal is mechanical. This bites hardest on fixed-threshold routes like the [UK's £1,270 Skilled Worker maintenance](/uk-skilled-worker-visa-maintenance-funds-1270-rule-from-nigeria) and UK student maintenance, where the balance must clear the line **every single day** of the 28-day window at the prevailing conversion.

**Rule: hold a 10–15% buffer above the converted requirement if your funds sit in naira.**

## When a Domiciliary Account Genuinely Helps

A dollar/pound/euro domiciliary account at a Nigerian commercial bank removes the volatility problem — $30,000 today is $30,000 at assessment. It helps most when:

*   **Your route has a hard threshold** (UK 28-day maintenance) and you want zero exchange-rate exposure
*   **Your funding originates abroad** — a sponsor remitting from the US/UK lands cleanly in a dom account with a paper trail matching the source
*   **You are showing large amounts** where a naira buffer would mean tying up millions extra

What a dom account does **not** do: launder credibility onto unexplained money. A sudden $25,000 cash lodgement into a dom account triggers exactly the same [source-of-funds scrutiny](/funds-parking-why-large-unexplained-deposits-get-nigerian-visas-refused) as a naira lump sum — arguably more, since dollar cash deposits invite extra questions.

## Side-by-Side

| Factor | Naira account | Domiciliary account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accepted by embassies | ✅ | ✅ |
| Exchange-rate risk | ⚠️ needs 10–15% buffer | ✅ none (if in target currency) |
| Ease of funding locally | ✅ easy | ⚠️ dollar sourcing needed |
| Statement history depth | Usually strong (daily use) | Often thin — dom accounts sit dormant |
| Verification | Standard | Standard |

That "thin history" row matters: an active naira salary account with six months of living on it tells a better [genuineness story](/how-do-embassies-verify-bank-statements-in-nigeria) than a dom account that woke up last month. Many strong files pair them — naira account for financial life, dom account for the headline balance — with both statements submitted.
