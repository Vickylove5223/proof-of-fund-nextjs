### Acceptable Ways to Show Proof of Funds

IRCC accepts several forms of documentation. You must prove the funds are available, genuine, and accessible for your studies without relying on unauthorized work in Canada. **Strong Options Include**:

*   Bank statements (4–6 months history from acceptable banks like Access, GTB, Zenith, UBA, etc.).
*   Fixed deposits or term deposits.
*   Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) from a participating Canadian bank (highly trusted).
*   Proof of paid first-year tuition/housing.
*   Education loan from a recognized lender.
*   Sponsor documents + notarized affidavit of support (with relationship proof, sponsor’s bank statements, employment letter, etc.).

**Important for Sponsors (Parents, Relatives, etc.)**:

*   Provide a clear sponsor letter/affidavit explaining the relationship and commitment.
*   Include sponsor’s recent statements, tax documents, and proof of income.
*   Funds should ideally show history — sudden large deposits raise red flags.

### Common Reasons Nigerian Applications Are Refused on Financial Grounds

*   Sudden large deposits without credible explanation (source of funds issues).
*   Inconsistent account activity or low average balance.
*   Using microfinance or fintech accounts (OPay, Kuda, etc.) — IRCC and Nigerian banks prefer CBN-licensed commercial banks.
*   Weak or missing sponsor documentation.
*   Showing funds that appear borrowed/temporary without proper disclosure.
