When you submit your bank statement for a visa or school application, every detail matters. But one of the most overlooked details is the transaction narration—that short description explaining where the money came from.

The wrong narration can raise red flags for consular officers, leading to questions or even outright rejection. Getting it right is crucial for a smooth application process.

At POF NG, we've seen how simple mistakes can cause major delays. That's why we’ve compiled this guide to the 12 best and most acceptable narrations for your Proof of Funds (POF) in Nigeria.

Using the right description tells a clear and believable story about your financial standing.

Why Your POF Narration Matters
------------------------------

A transaction narration is the story behind the money entering your account. For embassies, it’s a quick way to verify the legitimacy of your funds. A vague or suspicious narration like "Personal" or "Credit" for a large sum can make them doubt the source of your money. A clear, accurate narration, however, builds trust and supports your application.

A mismatch between the narration and your supporting documents is one of the key [reasons visa applications get rejected](http://afrikamombraids.local/why-visa-applications-get-rejected-because-of-pof-learn-how-to-avoid-it/). Consistency is key.

12 Embassy-Approved Narrations for Proof of Funds
-------------------------------------------------

Here are 12 clear and acceptable narrations to use for large deposits in your account when preparing your POF documents.

1.  **Liquidation of Investment**  
    Use this when you have sold financial assets like stocks, bonds, or treasury bills. It shows the funds came from a legitimate investment portfolio. You should have supporting documents from your stockbroker or investment firm.
2.  **Investment Proceeds**  
    This is slightly different from liquidation. It refers to profits or returns earned from an investment, not the sale of the entire asset. It shows a history of sound financial management.
3.  **Liquidation of Assets**  
    This applies when you sell a physical asset, such as a car or valuable equipment. You must have a receipt or deed of sale to back this up.
4.  **Assets Proceeds**  
    This is the narration for the money received from the sale described above. It directly links the deposit to the asset you sold.
5.  **Dividend Proceeds**  
    If you own shares in a company, use this narration for payments you receive as dividends. It’s a great way to show steady, passive income.
6.  **Salary**  
    For funds coming from your employer. This is the most common and easily verifiable source of income for many applicants. It demonstrates stable employment, which is a major plus for visa officers reviewing your application for countries like the [United Kingdom](https://www.gov.uk/browse/visas-immigration).
7.  **Sales of Property/Land**  
    This is ideal for very large, one-time deposits. You must have a signed Deed of Assignment or contract of sale as proof. This is a strong justification for a sudden increase in your account balance.
8.  **Payment for Goods/Services**  
    Perfect for business owners and freelancers. This narration shows the money is legitimate business income. You should be able to provide invoices or receipts if asked.

9.  **Gift from \[Sponsor's Name\]**  
    If a family member is sponsoring you, this is the correct narration. It must be accompanied by a [deed of gift](https://www.lawdepot.com/contracts/gift-deed/) and a letter from your sponsor. This is common for students applying for a [Canadian study visa](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents.html#doc3).
10.  **Educational Funds/Sponsorship**  
     This is a more specific version of the gift narration, used exclusively for academic purposes. It clearly states the fund's purpose, leaving no room for doubt.
11.  **IFO \[Customer's Name\]**  
     IFO stands for "In Favour Of." This is used when a third party makes a deposit into your account on behalf of someone else. For example, if a company pays a contractor through you. It provides clarity on the transaction flow.
12.  **Rent**  
     If you are a landlord, this narration is used for income from your properties. A tenancy agreement serves as excellent proof for this source of funds, which is accepted for applications like the [Schengen visa](https://schengenvisainfo.com/schengen-visa-application-requirements/proof-of-sufficient-funds/).
