When you’re applying for a visa, seeking admission to a university abroad, or making a large purchase like a house, you’ll often be asked for "Proof of Funds" (POF). This document shows you have the required money.

But what happens when they ask for a "Proof of Source of Funds"? This is a deeper question, and getting it right is crucial for your application's success.

While they sound similar, these two requests are very different. Understanding this difference is the first step toward a smooth and successful application process.

We're here to break it down for you.

Proof of Source of Funds (POSOF) is the evidence that shows the origin of the money you are using for a transaction or application. It's a paper trail that proves your funds were obtained legally and are not from illicit activities.

Essentially, it answers the question: "Where did this money come from?"

Understanding Proof of Source of Funds (POSOF)
----------------------------------------------

Think of it this way: Proof of Funds (POF) is a snapshot. It shows a bank balance on a specific day, proving you have the money.

Proof of Source of Funds (POSOF), on the other hand, is a story. It tells the journey of how that money landed in your account.

Institutions like embassies, banks, and universities require POSOF to comply with Anti-Money Laundering (AML) regulations and to ensure your financial stability is genuine. They want to see that the funds weren't just borrowed temporarily to pass a check.

They need to trust that your financial situation is legitimate and sustainable. uk/guidance/financial-evidence-for-student-and-child-student-route-applicants) has strict rules on financial evidence for visa applicants.

Common Examples of Acceptable Sources of Funds
----------------------------------------------

So, what counts as a valid source? The good news is that there are many acceptable ways to prove where your money came from.

Here are some of the most common examples and the documents you might need for each.

### Salary and Employment Income

This is one of the most straightforward sources. If your funds come from your job, you can prove it with documents like official payslips for the last few months, your employment contract, or a letter from your employer confirming your salary.

### Business Income or Dividends

For business owners, proving your source of funds involves showing your company's financial health. You can use audited company accounts, dividend statements, or tax returns that show the profits your business has generated.

### Sale of Property or Assets

Did you sell a house, a car, or another valuable asset? The documentation for this would include the signed deed of sale, a letter from the lawyer who handled the transaction, and bank statements showing the deposit from the sale.

### Gift from a Sponsor or Family Member

Receiving money as a gift is common, especially for students. To prove this, you'll need a signed gift deed or a letter from the person giving you the money.

This letter should clearly state their relationship to you, the amount, and that it is a gift with no expectation of repayment. You will also need to show the source of \*their\* funds.

For more details, it's wise to check the specific [proof of funds requirements](/guides/learn-about-proof-of-funds-requirements-in-nigeria) for your application.

### Investment Proceeds

If your money comes from stocks, bonds, or other investments, you can provide statements from your investment broker showing the liquidation of those assets. This demonstrates a clear and legal origin for the funds.
