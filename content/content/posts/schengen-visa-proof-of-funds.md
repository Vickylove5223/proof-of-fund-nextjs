When applying for a **Schengen visa**, one of the most important requirements is your **proof of funds (POF)**. This financial requirement shows that you can take care of yourself during your stay in Europe without becoming a financial burden.

But many applicants get **visa refusals** not because they don’t have money, but because of **how they present their proof of funds**. This article breaks down the **Schengen visa proof of funds requirement for Nigerians**, what mistakes to avoid, and answers to common questions.

### How Much Proof of Funds Do You Need for a Schengen Visa?

For a Schengen visa application, Nigerians generally need to show between **₦15 million to ₦20 million** as proof of funds. However, the **exact naira amount depends on the exchange rate**.

The higher the dollar or euro goes, the more money you need in naira. For example:

*   If today’s euro exchange rate increases, the required naira value also increases.
*   Always check the latest conversion rate before applying.

For a Schengen visa, you must prove you have access to **€45 to €120 per day** for each day of your planned trip, depending on your destination country. Additionally, you need to provide a **3 to 6-month bank statement** and guarantee you have enough funds for your return journey to Nigeria.

Specific proof of funds rules depend on the exact country you are visiting:

Minimum Daily Reference Amounts (Per Person)

*   **France:** €32.50/day (with proof of paid accommodation) or €120/day (without).
*   **Germany, Belgium & The Netherlands:** €45/day.
*   **Italy:** Fixed amounts apply (e.g., about €270 for 1-5 days, plus a scaled daily amount).
*   **Spain:** €122/day, with an absolute minimum required per application regardless of duration.

Requirements for Nigerian Applicants

*   **Account Statement:** Must be printed in A4 format on the bank's letterhead, and carry the official stamp and signature of the bank. 
*   **Income Evidence:** If employed, you must provide your last 3-6 months of payslips and salary bank statements. 
*   **Total Balance:** For a standard 2-week trip, it is highly recommended to have a balance between **₦1.5 million to ₦3.5 million** (depending on the exchange rate and the cost of your prepaid flights/hotels), to ensure you easily pass the minimum threshold of the destination country. 
*   **Sponsorship:** If you are being sponsored, your sponsor must provide an official invitation letter, their bank statements, and proof of legal residency in the destination country. 
*   **Travel Insurance:** You must also buy [Travel Medical Insurance](https://nigeria.blsspainvisa.com/assets/pdf/checklist-tourist.pdf) covering all Schengen countries with a minimum medical coverage of €30,000.

### Why Bank Statements Are More Important Than Just Having Money

A common mistake applicants make is **depositing a large lump sum** of ₦15–₦20 million into their account just before applying. This is a **red flag** because:

*   Embassies assume you **borrowed the money** just to apply for the visa
*   They want to see **financial history, not sudden wealth**

Instead, what matters most is your **bank statement activity**. Your account should show:

*   **Regular inflows** (salary, business income, rent, etc.)
*   **Reasonable outflows** (living expenses, business expenses)
*   **Consistent financial activity over 3–6 months**

If at the end of the statement period you have a **healthy balance of ₦15–₦20 million**, your application is much stronger than if you only just deposited the money. ![](https://img.freepik.com/free-photo/businessman-checking-bankbook_53876-40975.jpg?uid=R45719163&ga=GA1.1.1313607400.1731226079&semt=ais_hybrid&w=740&q=80)

### Schengen Tourist Visa Daily Proof of Funds

If you’re applying for a **tourist visa**, embassies also calculate based on **daily living expenses**. For Spain, for example, you need at least **€118 per day**.

So, if you’re staying for **10 days**: €118 × 10 = **€1,180 (approx. ₦2 million depending on exchange rate)** This is separate from your flight and accommodation costs.

### Common Mistakes That Lead to Visa Rejection

1.  **Sudden deposits** of large amounts without explanation
2.  Using an **inactive bank account** with no history
3.  Submitting **only a sponsor’s account** without proper documentation
4.  Not meeting the **daily expense requirement** for tourist visas
5.  Providing **unofficial bank statements** without a verification letter

### Final Thoughts

The embassy is not just checking if you have money—they want to confirm that the money is **truly yours, stable, and traceable**. To avoid rejection:Use an **active bank account** with regular inflows and outflows

*   Maintain a balance of **₦15–₦20 million** depending on the exchange rate
*   Provide **official bank statements and verification letters**
*   Avoid sudden unexplained deposits

By preparing your proof of funds correctly, you significantly increase your chances of getting your Schengen visa approved.

### FAQs About Schengen Visa Proof of Funds

**1\. Can one use a joint account as proof of funds?** Yes, but you must provide documents proving you are a joint account holder and have access to the funds. **2\. Can I use a sponsor’s account statement?** Yes, but sponsors are usually limited to **immediate family members** (parents, siblings, spouse). You’ll also need supporting documents like a sponsor’s letter and proof of relationship. **3\. What if someone sponsored my travel, do I still need proof of funds?** Yes, even with a sponsor, embassies may still request your personal financial documents. A sponsor’s account alone is not always enough. **4\. For a tourist visa, is it really €118 per day?** Yes, that’s the standard calculation for Spain and some other Schengen countries. Multiply **€118 by the number of days you plan to stay** to know your requirement. **5\. What if the funds came from selling property?** That’s acceptable, but you must provide **supporting documents** (sales agreement, receipts, proof of ownership, transfer evidence). **6\. Can ₦5 million take me to Spain for work?** No. For a work visa or even a tourist visa, ₦5 million is generally not enough. You need at least **₦15–₦20 million** depending on your purpose of travel and exchange rates.
